Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)
Overview

Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) is a cardiovascular condition caused by the buildup of atherosclerotic plaque within the coronary arteries. The narrowing of these arteries reduces blood flow and oxygen supply to the heart muscle. CAD is one of the leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide.

Symptoms

Common symptoms of CAD include:

Chest pain (angina)
Shortness of breath
Fatigue
Dizziness
Nausea
Pain radiating to the neck, jaw, shoulder, or arm

Some patients may have atypical symptoms or remain asymptomatic.

Risk Factors
Non-Modifiable Risk Factors

Male gender

Genetic predisposition
Modifiable Risk Factors
Hypertension
High cholesterol
Smoking
Obesity
Diabetes mellitus
Physical inactivity
Unhealthy diet
Stress
Pathophysiology

CAD develops when fatty deposits accumulate in the walls of coronary arteries.

Process:

Endothelial injury
LDL cholesterol deposition
Foam cell formation
Plaque development
Arterial narrowing
Reduced blood flow to the myocardium

Plaque rupture may lead to blood clot formation and acute myocardial infarction.

Diagnosis

Diagnostic methods include:

Electrocardiogram (ECG)

Used to detect abnormal electrical activity and signs of ischemia.

Echocardiography

Evaluates heart structure and function.

Stress Testing

Assesses heart performance during exercise or induced stress.

Coronary Angiography

Gold standard for identifying coronary artery blockages.

Blood Tests
Troponin
Lipid profile
C-reactive protein
Blood glucose
Treatment
Lifestyle Modification
Smoking cessation
Weight management
Healthy diet
Regular exercise
Blood pressure control
Diabetes management
Medications
Aspirin
Statins
Beta-blockers
ACE inhibitors
Nitroglycerin
Interventional Procedures
Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI)

Insertion of coronary stents to improve blood flow.

Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG)

Surgical bypass of blocked coronary arteries.

Complications

Untreated CAD may lead to:

Myocardial infarction (heart attack)
Heart failure
Arrhythmias
Mitral regurgitation
Sudden cardiac death
Prevention
Maintain healthy cholesterol levels
Control blood pressure
Avoid smoking
Exercise regularly
Maintain healthy body weight
Manage diabetes
Follow a heart-healthy diet
