Pneumonia
Overview

Pneumonia is an infection that causes inflammation of the air sacs (alveoli) in one or both lungs. The air sacs may fill with fluid or pus, making breathing difficult and reducing oxygen exchange. Pneumonia can be caused by bacteria, viruses, or fungi and ranges from mild to life-threatening.

Symptoms

Common symptoms of pneumonia include:

Cough
Fever
Chills
Shortness of breath
Chest pain during breathing or coughing
Fatigue
Rapid breathing
Increased heart rate
Low oxygen saturation

In severe cases, patients may experience confusion, respiratory distress, or cyanosis.

Risk Factors
Non-Modifiable Risk Factors
Age over 65 years
Infants and young children
Chronic medical conditions
Weakened immune system
Modifiable Risk Factors
Smoking
Excessive alcohol use
Poor nutrition
Exposure to air pollution
Lack of vaccination
Causes

Pneumonia may be caused by:

Bacterial Pneumonia
Streptococcus pneumoniae
Haemophilus influenzae
Viral Pneumonia
Influenza virus
Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV)
Coronavirus
Fungal Pneumonia
Histoplasma
Pneumocystis jirovecii
Diagnosis

Diagnostic methods include:

Chest X-Ray

Used to identify lung infiltrates and areas of infection.

Physical Examination

Assessment of breathing sounds and respiratory status.

Blood Tests
White blood cell count
Inflammatory markers
Blood cultures
Pulse Oximetry

Measures oxygen saturation levels.

Sputum Analysis

Identifies infectious organisms.

Treatment
Lifestyle and Supportive Care
Adequate rest
Hydration
Oxygen support when required
Medications
Bacterial Pneumonia
Antibiotics
Viral Pneumonia
Antiviral medications
Supportive treatment
Severe Cases
Hospitalization
Intravenous fluids
Mechanical ventilation
Complications

Untreated pneumonia may lead to:

Respiratory failure
Sepsis
Lung abscess
Pleural effusion
Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)
Prevention
Annual influenza vaccination
Pneumococcal vaccination
Hand hygiene
Smoking cessation
Healthy lifestyle
Early treatment of respiratory infections
